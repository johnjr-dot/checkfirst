The 3-step check before you share

Takes under a minute. Copy it and send it to family or a group chat.

  1. Who is saying this? Go to their official page or website yourself. Don't use the link you were sent.
  2. Is anyone else reporting it? Search the headline. Look for two outlets you already trust.
  3. Is the photo or video original? Run a reverse image search (Google Lens, TinEye) and see where it first appeared.

Warning signs to know

Fake videos and voices

Lips slightly out of sync, flat voice, blurry mouth or teeth, and no source. If a famous person offers free money, it's fake.

AI-made images

Odd hands or fingers, garbled text on signs, shadows pointing different ways, skin that looks too smooth.

Scam messages

Pressure to act now, a request to send money back, a link to "confirm" your details, or a number you don't recognise.

Old news, new story

Real photo, wrong story. Check the date and place it first appeared before you believe the caption.
